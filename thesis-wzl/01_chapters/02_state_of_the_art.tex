\chapter{State of the Art in Whole-Body Motion Planning for Mobile
  Manipulators}
\label{ch:sota}

\Cref{ch:introduction} argues that the Line-less Mobile Assembly System
(LMAS) setting calls for whole-body motion planning rather than a
decoupled base--arm pipeline, and states the resulting requirements R1
to R7 in \cref{tab:requirements}. This chapter surveys how the
literature approaches the problem and narrows the field down to the
framework used in the remainder of this thesis.
\Cref{sec:sota-classification} introduces a classification of
motion-planning approaches for mobile manipulators.
\Cref{sec:sota-holistic} then focuses on \emph{holistic} (whole-body)
planners, presenting the \ocs{} framework (\cref{sec:sota-ocs2}) and the
main alternatives (\cref{sec:sota-alternatives}).
\Cref{sec:sota-selection} scores the candidates against
\cref{tab:requirements} and justifies the selection of \ocs{}.

\section{Classification of Motion Planning Approaches}
\label{sec:sota-classification}

Motion planning for mobile manipulators can be organised along a few
largely orthogonal axes. The two published reviews of the field
distinguish approaches primarily by \emph{how base and arm are coupled}
and by \emph{the algorithmic principle} used to generate
motion~\cite{Sandakalum2022Survey,Thakar2023MobileManip}; a third,
practical axis concerns \emph{when the computation happens}.
\Cref{fig:sota-taxonomy} shows how the axes overlap and where the
families discussed below fall.

\paragraph{Decoupled versus whole-body.}
The first and, for this work, most important distinction is between
\emph{decoupled} and \emph{whole-body} schemes. A decoupled scheme plans
base and arm separately and in sequence: a navigation stack drives the
base to a stand-off pose, after which an arm planner solves the
manipulation task from that fixed base configuration. A whole-body
scheme instead treats base and arm as a \emph{single} redundant
kinematic (or dynamic) system and solves for their motion jointly, so
that the task-space objective is distributed between base and arm by the
planner itself. This is the formulation used by the holistic reactive
controller of Haviland et~al.~\cite{Haviland2022Holistic} and by the
whole-body \mpc{} of Minniti
et~al.~\cite{Minniti2019WholeBodyMPC}; it generalises null-space
redundancy resolution from a fixed base~\cite{Dietrich2015NullSpace} to
the combined system. As set out in \cref{sec:need-wholebody}, decoupling
sacrifices reach, solution quality, and the ability to revise the base
decision, at the price of a higher-dimensional and more tightly coupled
problem.

\paragraph{Algorithmic principle.}
Independently of the coupling, the motion itself can be produced by
different categories of algorithm.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sampling-based planning} explores the configuration
        space by randomly sampling and connecting collision-free states,
        as in Probabilistic Roadmaps~\cite{Kavraki1996PRM} and
        Rapidly-exploring Random Trees~\cite{LaValle2001RRT}, both of
        which are exposed through OMPL and, in turn, through
        MoveIt~\cite{Sucan2012OMPL,moveit2}. These methods are effective
        at finding feasible paths in cluttered, high-dimensional spaces
        and are probabilistically complete, but the path they return is
        feasible rather than optimal with respect to a running cost, and
        the computation is normally performed before execution rather
        than inside a control loop~\cite{LaValle2006Planning}.
  \item \textbf{Optimisation-based planning} formulates motion as a
        constrained optimisation over a trajectory. Solving that
        optimisation once, before execution, over the complete
        trajectory is \emph{trajectory optimisation}; re-solving it
        online over a receding horizon and applying only its first part
        turns the same formulation into \emph{Model Predictive Control}
        (\mpc{})~\cite{Rawlings2017MPC,Mayne2000Constrained}. Both
        variants handle joint, velocity, and collision limits as
        explicit constraints and return smooth motion that is optimal
        with respect to the stated cost.
  \item \textbf{Reactive schemes} compute an instantaneous command from
        the current state, for example through potential
        fields~\cite{Khatib1986PotentialFields} or through a quadratic
        program solved once per control step
        (QP-IK)~\cite{Haviland2021NEO}. A single quadratic program is
        cheaper to solve than an optimisation over a horizon of the same
        model, which is why these schemes run at control rate on modest
        hardware; the price is that they optimise only the current
        instant and therefore cannot trade a worse command now for a
        feasible one later.
  \item \textbf{Learning-based schemes} replace or augment the planner
        with a policy learned by (deep) reinforcement learning, mapping
        observations directly to base and arm
        commands~\cite{Honerkamp2023N2M2,Jauhri2022Reachability}.
        Learning-based schemes move the cost of whole-body reasoning
        from run time to training time, but they raise questions of data
        efficiency, of reproducibility, and of transfer from simulation
        to hardware~\cite{Iriondo2023DRL,Kang2024DoorOpening}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Offline versus online.}
Finally, a planner may operate \emph{offline}, computing a complete
trajectory before execution, or \emph{online}, re-planning continuously
as new state estimates and targets arrive. It is the online,
feedback-driven variant that supplies the disturbance rejection required
in a shared, changing LMAS workspace, because an offline trajectory
carries no mechanism for reacting to a scene that has moved since it was
computed~\cite{Finean2023ContextAware}. Whole-body \mpc{} sits at the
intersection of the \emph{whole-body}, \emph{optimisation-based}, and
\emph{online} categories (\cref{fig:sota-taxonomy}), which is the region
of the design space this thesis targets.

\section{Overview of Holistic Motion Planners}
\label{sec:sota-holistic}

\emph{Holistic} (whole-body) planners solve for base and arm motion in a
single formulation. The mature, deployable representatives of this class
are predominantly optimisation-based, because expressing the task as a
cost and the physical restrictions as constraints maps directly onto the
coupled, redundant, and constrained nature of the mobile-manipulation
problem. Among open-source stacks that couple a navigation stack with a
genuine whole-body controller, three integration mechanisms recur:
replacing the Nav2 local planner through the
\texttt{nav2\_core::Controller} plugin interface, wrapping the whole-body
controller in \texttt{ros2\_control} as
\texttt{wbc\_ros} does~\cite{wbcros,ros2control}, and bridging the
navigation stack's velocity and goal topics to a whole-body solver while
the robot state reaches the solver through the joint-state publisher, as
in the WoLF stack built on \ocs{}~\cite{wolfsetup}. On the algorithmic
side the same set of stacks is dominated by two formulations: optimal
control over a horizon~\cite{Sleiman2021UnifiedMPC,Pankert2020PerceptiveMPC}
and constraint-based task-space control solved as a quadratic
program~\cite{Stelter2022OpenSource,Stueben2021ConstraintWBC}.

\Cref{fig:mpc-receding} recalls how the receding-horizon mechanism at
the core of the first formulation works. The following subsections
describe \ocs{} and then position the principal alternatives against it.

\subsection{The \ocs{} Framework: Optimal-Control-Based Model Predictive
  Control}
\label{sec:sota-ocs2}

\ocs{} (\emph{Optimal Control for Switched Systems}) is an open-source
library written in C++ for numerical optimal control and developed at
the Robotic Systems Lab, ETH Z\"urich. It provides solvers for
continuous-time optimal-control problems, most notably Sequential
Linear-Quadratic (SLQ) control, a constrained variant of differential
dynamic programming, together with the infrastructure required to run
them as an \mpc{} controller; the solver was introduced for quadrupedal
locomotion and later extended with a feedback policy and with relaxed
barrier functions for
constraints~\cite{Farshidian2017SLQ,Grandia2019FeedbackMPC,%
Feller2017RelaxedBarrier,ocs2Toolbox}.

Three properties make \ocs{} suitable for whole-body mobile
manipulation; \cref{fig:ocs2-structure} shows how they fit together.
First, the derivatives of cost and constraints that the solver needs are
obtained by \emph{automatic differentiation}: the model (kinematics,
cost terms, constraints) is supplied symbolically, and \ocs{} generates
and compiles the corresponding derivative code through
CppAD/CppADCodeGen, so that a change of model does not require hand-derived
Jacobians. Second, a \emph{reference manager} allows the desired
task-space trajectory to be replaced while the solver is running, which
is the interface a target-generating node needs in order to steer the
controller. Third, and decisive for a real-time deployment, \ocs{}
separates optimisation from execution through an MPC--MRT split: the
\mpc{} process solves the optimal-control problem at its own rate and
publishes a \emph{policy}, while a lightweight Model Reference Tracking
(MRT) client evaluates that policy at the control rate to produce the
instantaneous command. The split makes it possible to run optimiser and
hardware interface as separate processes, even on separate machines, a
property this thesis exploits in the distributed architecture of
\cref{ch:integration}.

\ocs{} has been used for whole-body mobile manipulation across several
platforms. The kinematic planner of Giftthaler et~al.\ addresses exactly
the non-holonomic base considered here~\cite{Giftthaler2017Kinematic};
Minniti et~al.\ demonstrate whole-body \mpc{} on a dynamically stable
mobile manipulator~\cite{Minniti2019WholeBodyMPC} and extend it to
force-controlled interaction with the
environment~\cite{Minniti2021Interaction}; Pankert and Hutter run
continuous mobile manipulation from onboard
perception~\cite{Pankert2020PerceptiveMPC}; Sleiman et~al.\ unify
locomotion and manipulation in one \mpc{}
formulation~\cite{Sleiman2021UnifiedMPC}; and Mittal et~al.\ apply the
same toolbox to articulated objects in unknown
scenes~\cite{Mittal2022Articulated}. This record covers R1 to R4 of
\cref{tab:requirements} on hardware of comparable complexity, and the
process-separable architecture speaks to R5, which is why \ocs{} is
carried forward as the leading candidate for \hero{}.

\subsection{Alternative Frameworks and Methods}
\label{sec:sota-alternatives}

Several alternatives to \ocs{} exist, spanning the algorithmic
categories of \cref{sec:sota-classification}.

\paragraph{Sampling-based planning (MoveIt).}
MoveIt~\cite{moveit2} is the de-facto standard motion-planning framework
in the ROS ecosystem, exposing the OMPL sampling-based
planners~\cite{Sucan2012OMPL}. Its strength is collision-free planning
for redundant arms in cluttered scenes, computed before execution; it
can in principle be extended to a mobile base by treating the base pose
as additional planning variables. In its open-source form, however,
MoveIt underpins the \emph{decoupled} navigate-then-manipulate pipeline
and provides no online, coordinated base--arm feedback of the kind R1
and R4 ask for. Whole-body capabilities for mobile manipulators are
offered by the commercial MoveIt Pro platform, which is not open source
and therefore does not satisfy R7~\cite{moveitpro}. MoveIt is
consequently the incumbent pipeline from which this work departs rather
than a competing whole-body planner; a quantitative comparison of the
two pipelines requires a second, decoupled implementation on the same
platform and is left to future work (\cref{ch:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Other optimisation-based and \mpc{} tools.}
Beyond \ocs{}, whole-body behaviour can be realised with constraint-based
task-space controllers driven by a solver over a short horizon. Stelter
et~al.\ publish an open-source framework for mobile manipulators that
casts the task-space constraints as a linear \mpc{}
problem~\cite{Stelter2022OpenSource}, and Stueben et~al.\ apply
constraint-based whole-body control to mobile manipulators in
human-centred environments~\cite{Stueben2021ConstraintWBC}. The
\texttt{wbc\_ros} package wraps a quadratic-program whole-body controller
in \texttt{ros2\_control}~\cite{wbcros}. These tools satisfy R1 and R2
but either target a different base morphology or expose a shorter
effective horizon than a full optimal-control formulation, and the
integration effort for a differential-drive base under ROS~2 is
comparable to the one reported here.

\paragraph{Reactive quadratic-program inverse kinematics.}
QP-based inverse kinematics resolves the \ee{} task and secondary
objectives---joint-limit avoidance, manipulability---into joint
velocities at each control step, and extends naturally to base and arm
together~\cite{Haviland2021NEO,Haviland2022Holistic}. Burgess-Limerick
et~al.\ show that this class of controller supports manipulation while
the base is moving~\cite{BurgessLimerick2024ReactiveBase}, so it meets
R1, R3, and R4. Because it optimises a single step, however, it cannot
anticipate a constraint that will become active later in the motion,
which is what R2 asks for over a horizon.

\paragraph{Learning-based whole-body control.}
A growing body of work hybridises classical navigation with
reinforcement learning, training policies that map observations directly
to base and arm commands. Honerkamp et~al.\ learn a navigation policy
that supports arbitrary mobile-manipulation motions in unseen
environments~\cite{Honerkamp2023N2M2}; Jauhri et~al.\ shape exploration
with reachability priors~\cite{Jauhri2022Reachability}; Iriondo
et~al.\ learn base-positioning policies for manipulation
operations~\cite{Iriondo2023DRL}; and Kang et~al.\ combine a learned
policy with adaptive position-force control for door
opening~\cite{Kang2024DoorOpening}. These approaches satisfy R1 and R4
and are cheap at run time, but the training data, the reproducibility of
the resulting policy, and the transfer from simulation to hardware
conflict with R7 and with the near-term, hardware-first goal of this
work.

\section{Comparison of Motion Planning Algorithms and Selection of a
  Suitable Algorithm}
\label{sec:sota-selection}

The choice of a planner for \hero{} follows the requirements R1 to R7
stated in \cref{tab:requirements}: coordinated whole-body motion,
explicit handling of joint, velocity, non-holonomic and self-collision
constraints, deliberate redundancy resolution, online re-planning from
the measured state, real-time feasibility on the on-board computer,
ROS~2 interfaces, and a controller whose behaviour can be analysed on
hardware. \Cref{tab:sota-comparison} scores the four candidate families
against those criteria. The scores are judgements, not measurements:
each one is read off the published evidence cited in
\cref{sec:sota-holistic,sec:sota-alternatives} for that family, and no
row rests on a trial run of the alternatives on \hero{}. Only the
\ocs{} column is later confirmed by measurement, in
\cref{ch:hardware}.

Sampling-based planning is mature and well supported but computes its
trajectory before execution and, in its standard form, keeps base and
arm separate, which disqualifies it as the primary whole-body planner
while keeping it relevant as the incumbent alternative. Reactive QP-IK
meets every requirement except the horizon in R2. Learning-based control
is attractive in principle but conflicts with R7 and with the near-term,
hardware-first goals of this work. Whole-body \mpc{} is the only family
that satisfies R1 to R4 simultaneously; its weak point is R5, the
computational burden of solving an optimal-control problem online, which
is addressed through the MPC--MRT split and a distributed deployment
(\cref{ch:integration}) and quantified experimentally in
\cref{ch:hardware}.

Among whole-body \mpc{} implementations, \ocs{} is selected for its
open-source implementation, its explicit constraint support, its
demonstrated use on wheeled and legged mobile manipulators
(\cref{sec:sota-ocs2}), and, decisive for a real-time deployment on
distributed hardware, its process-separable MPC--MRT architecture. The
remainder of this thesis therefore integrates and validates \ocs{}-based
whole-body \mpc{} on the \hero{} platform.
